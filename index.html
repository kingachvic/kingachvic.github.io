Study medicine from one organised place

MiC Study is a study app for medical students. It keeps your lecture slides and textbooks, your notes and handwriting, your flashcards and your revision plan together, and syncs them between your devices through your own Google Drive.

It's made by a medical student at Kenyatta University, for the way medical school is actually studied: read a topic, make notes, test yourself, and come back to it before it fades.

What it does

Library

PDFs, slides, Word files and YouTube lectures, grouped by unit. PowerPoint files are converted to PDF so they open anywhere.

Read and mark up

A fast PDF reader with search, a pen, a highlighter, and one tap to send highlighted text to your notes.

Notes and handwriting

Typed notes with headings and lists, and handwriting with a stylus on the same page.

Flashcards and quizzes

Cards and questions scheduled with FSRS spaced repetition, so you review each one just before you'd forget it.

Revision plan

Each topic gets a first read and spaced revisions. Exams pull revisions forward, and a readiness score shows what's weakest.

Today

Today's sessions, cards due and the next exam, in the app and on a home-screen widget, with a Pomodoro timer and study log.

How MiC Study uses your Google account

Signing in with Google is optional. If you do, MiC Study asks for one permission: to see and manage only the Google Drive files it creates or that you open with it (the drive.file permission). It can't see anything else in your Drive.

It uses that permission to:

  • keep a MiC Study folder in your Drive with a copy of your notes, cards, schedule and library files, so your phone, tablet and other devices stay in step;
  • convert PowerPoint files you add into PDFs, using Google Slides in your own Drive.

Where your files are kept

  • Always on your device. The app works without an account and without the internet.
  • If you sign in, a copy also goes to the MiC Study folder in your Drive, and your other devices read from it.
  • MiC Study does not bring in the files already in your Drive, because it isn't allowed to see them. You add a file to the Library once, and from then on it is on every device you sign in on.

Your study data goes from your device to your own Google Drive and back. There is no MiC Study server, so the developer never receives it. Details are in the privacy policy.

Getting the app

On a laptop or desktop: open MiC Study in your browser (Chrome or Edge). To keep it like a program, choose Install in its Settings: it then opens in its own window and works offline.

On Android phones and tablets (Android 10 or later): the app is being tested with a small group of students.

Sign in with the same Google account on each device and your library, notes and cards follow you.
